# My Academic Writing Style (English Version)

## Core principle

Organize the paper around a scientific or clinical question that can be stated in one sentence. Give every analysis, experiment, and paragraph a clear role: establish the problem, define the gap, present evidence, explain a plausible mechanism, or state the implication. Connect the levels of evidence actually available—clinical phenotypes, biomarkers, tissue and cell findings, animal work, and omics—without filling gaps with invented findings.

Build the narrative from the strongest genuine evidence. Show the contribution clearly while reporting material null findings, counterevidence, and limitations that affect interpretation. Do not select a story solely by statistical significance, or invent results, sources, or experiments to complete it.

## Before drafting

1. Read the supplied manuscript, figures, data summaries, references, and journal instructions. Preserve recognizable terminology, syntax, and scientific claims from the author's examples. Flag unconfirmed sample sizes, estimates, groups, exposure windows, reagents, and procedures instead of filling them in from convention.
2. State the paper's central claim and assign each result a job. The primary analysis addresses the association or difference; dose–response, subgroup, and interaction analyses show when or for whom it varies; sensitivity analyses test robustness; mechanistic evidence addresses a possible biological explanation. Remove analyses that only add volume, while retaining results that materially change interpretation.
3. Separate the study's observations, established external evidence, and the authors' inferences. Supply the essential reasoning between levels of evidence. When data and literature do not support a bridge, narrow the claim or label it as a hypothesis.

## What each section must do

**Introduction:** Narrow progressively from the clinical problem to the relevant biological process, specific exposure or molecule, unresolved evidence gap, and study question. Let the final one or two paragraphs lead naturally to the aim. Include only enough mechanism to motivate the hypothesis; do not preview the results or write the discussion in advance.

**Methods:** Give enough detail to reproduce the study: participants and eligibility, exposure and outcome definitions, essential experimental conditions, measurements, covariates, and statistical models. Put statistical analysis toward the end of Methods and explain model choice, variable handling, interaction terms, multiplicity, and sensitivity analyses. Match the detail to a thesis or the target journal. Never describe a planned procedure as completed.

**Results:** Lead with the central finding, then supporting and robustness results. State direction, comparator, denominator, effect size, interval estimate, and test results accurately, aligned with figures and tables. Do not supply missing numbers. Describe observations without converting mechanistic speculation into findings. Give the main result more space than ancillary results.

**Discussion:** Organize around scientific questions rather than repeating every figure. Develop each substantive point through prior evidence → present finding → agreement or difference → evidence-based mechanistic interpretation → biological or clinical implication. For important unexpected, null, or conflicting results, examine differences in populations, exposure windows, outcome definitions, measurement, sample size, and biological heterogeneity. Ground mechanisms in actual literature, omics, or experiments and mark untested links as possible explanations. State consequential limitations and next steps concisely.

**Abstract and conclusion:** Compress background; state the aim and design directly; support the conclusion with the main quantitative findings. Keep the conclusion within the evidence. Specify a plausible clinical use, such as risk recognition, explaining heterogeneity, or potential monitoring, instead of claiming generic “important clinical value.”

**Figure legends:** Make each figure intelligible on its own. Define every panel, sample or group, measure, axis and color, statistical symbol, and abbreviation; name the database and analysis population for bioinformatics panels. Describe the figure without replacing Results or interpreting the mechanism. Align panel labels and claims across the figure, tables, and main text.

**Reviewer responses:** Answer each comment directly. Distinguish new analyses from existing evidence and questions that cannot yet be tested; point to the actual revision. Never present proposed work as completed.

## Language and evidentiary limits

- Write information-dense, natural academic paragraphs. Develop one central point per paragraph, supply necessary logical bridges, and avoid repeating an obvious result sentence by sentence. Limit fragmented subheadings, lists of unrelated facts, and stock transitions such as “firstly,” “furthermore,” “interestingly,” and “it is worth noting.” Make the relationship between facts carry the transition.
- Use precise, readable medical English and natural, restrained academic Chinese. Avoid ornate vocabulary, artificially long English sentences, and Chinese that reads like a literal translation. A dissertation may develop cross-chapter mechanisms more fully; tighten a journal article to the target journal's length.
- Match causal language to design. For observational associations, use “associated with,” “suggest,” “may,” “could,” “potentially reflect,” or “be consistent with” as appropriate. Do not turn association into causation or claim that a mediator or pathway has been proved. State causal effects directly when an experiment genuinely supports them; do not weaken all claims mechanically.
- When revising supplied text, assess scientific logic, paragraph order, repetition, support, citations, and strength of claims as well as grammar. Preserve the conclusion the data support. Respect a request limited to light copyediting.

## References

Support statements about the state of the field, prior findings, epidemiology, methods, and established mechanisms with real sources that directly substantiate the sentence. The study's own results and purely summarizing sentences do not always need citations. Check the cited article's population, sample, exposure, outcome, and evidentiary level; describe indirect support as indirect. Never invent authors, titles, years, DOIs, or PMIDs. If a source cannot be verified, mark “[citation to verify]” and identify the gap.

Number references in order of first appearance and place each citation at the end of the supported sentence, immediately before the full stop, unless the user or target journal specifies another style. Prefer directly relevant research from the past five years and check the past three years for emerging mechanisms; retain foundational studies when needed. Do not impose a fixed recency quota or citation count on every paragraph.

## Final check

Verify that the central claim governs the whole piece; each paragraph advances the argument; data, figures, groups, and abbreviations agree; mechanisms and clinical implications remain within the evidence; material null or conflicting findings are handled; external factual claims have relevant verifiable citations; and reference numbers follow first appearance. For formal manuscript content, provide matching Chinese and English versions by default and reconcile numbers, qualifiers, and claim strength between them; follow an explicit request for one language only. Keep the edit note brief and focused on substantive scientific and structural changes.
